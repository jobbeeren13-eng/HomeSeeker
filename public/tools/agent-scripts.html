Agent Scripts

Word-for-word scripts for every stage of dealing with Dutch real estate agents (makelaars) - from first contact to bid submission. In English and Dutch.

First contact with the selling agent

When calling or emailing about a listing. Your goal: express serious interest without sounding desperate, and secure a viewing slot.

Script - calling the agent
"Good morning, my name is [your name]. I am calling about the property at [address] - I saw it on Funda this morning and I am very interested. I have a permanent contract, I earn [X] times the monthly rent, and all my documents are ready to send today. Is it possible to schedule a viewing? I am available any day this week."
Why this works: You immediately signal the three things Dutch agents care about most - income qualification, document readiness, and availability. Being first and prepared beats enthusiasm alone.
If they say all viewing slots are full
"I completely understand - it sounds like there is a lot of interest. Would it be possible to put me on a waiting list in case a slot opens? Or if another viewer cancels, I can be there within an hour. I would genuinely not want to miss this one."
Avoid: Never say "I love it from the photos" or describe the apartment as "perfect" before you have seen it. This weakens your negotiating position.

At the viewing

How to signal that you are a serious buyer (or tenant) without sounding desperate or scripted.

Opening the conversation
"Thanks for having me. I read the listing carefully before coming - I noticed [specific detail from the listing, e.g. the south-facing garden / the original 1920s details / the quiet street]. That is specifically what drew me here. A few practical questions when you have a moment."
Signalling you are a serious buyer
"I want to be upfront: I have my mortgage pre-approval confirmed, so there is no uncertainty on the financing side. I am also flexible on the transfer date - whatever works for the seller. If everything checks out today I would be in a position to submit an offer quickly."
5 behaviours that impress Dutch agents:
1. Arrive 5 minutes early. Be first. Agents notice.
2. Reference one specific detail from the listing description.
3. State your documents or mortgage approval are ready.
4. Ask specific, practical questions - not generic ones.
5. Do not bring children or pets to a first viewing unless the listing welcomes families.

After the viewing

Send within 1 hour of leaving. This follow-up is one of the highest-impact moves you can make - most viewers do not send one.

Thank-you / follow-up message (send within 1 hour)
"Hi [agent name], thank you for showing me the property at [address] today. I am very interested and would like to submit a formal offer. My documents are ready and my financing is confirmed. Please let me know what the next step is."
Asking about other offers without sounding weak
"Could you tell me roughly how many offers you are expecting, or what the seller's timeline for deciding looks like? I want to make sure I structure my offer appropriately."

During bid negotiation

Scripts for the most common negotiation moments. Confidence without showing your ceiling.

Responding to a counter-offer
"Thank you for the counter. Let me think about that and come back to you by [specific time - same day]. Can you confirm that the seller is still considering offers, and whether there are any other bids currently on the table at this level?"
Asking what price would secure it without showing your budget
"I want to make a serious offer - not one that gets rejected on a technicality. Without asking you to break any confidentiality, is there a price level that the seller has indicated would result in acceptance?"
Handling 'we have another offer at the same level'
"I understand. Can I ask - is the decision purely on price, or are other factors like transfer date and conditions also being weighted? I may be able to improve my offer on one or more of those dimensions."
Do not do these things:
- Do not offer to pay 3 months rent upfront as a substitute for income qualification. Illegal to demand, looks desperate to offer.
- Do not threaten to walk away unless you mean it. Dutch agents will let you walk.
- Do not cite Funda price comparisons without very solid data from the same building or street.
- Do not negotiate only by email. Call first, then confirm the agreed terms in writing.

Should you hire a buyer's agent (aankoopmakelaar)?

If you keep losing bids, a buyer's agent gives you access to off-market deals, negotiation expertise, and local market knowledge that is very hard to replicate as an expat.

Typical cost: EUR 2,500-4,500 flat fee or 1-1.5% of purchase price. Worth it in competitive markets like Amsterdam, Utrecht, and Haarlem where losing one bid costs you more in time and stress than the fee.

What to ask before hiring:
- How many properties did you help buyers acquire in this neighbourhood in the last 12 months?
- Do you have access to off-market listings or pre-market properties?
- What is your typical win rate on bids you submit?
- Can I speak with a previous client?
